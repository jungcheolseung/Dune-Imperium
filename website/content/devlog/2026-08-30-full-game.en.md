The engine in [the previous entry]({{< relref path="devlog/2026-08-14-engine-foundation.md" lang="en" >}}) could run one round, but most card effects were empty. From August 19 to 30 the cards were filled in one at a time, the structure was changed once in the middle, and at the end a whole game was connected to the learning environment. Some days have no commits, so the work is concentrated on August 19–20 and 26–30.

## 63 personal cards, one card at a time

There are 63 identities: 7 starting cards, 2 Reserve cards, and 54 shared Imperium cards (50 base-game cards and 4 CHOAM-only cards). Each card got a pair of commits: `Play <card>` for the implementation and `Document <card>` for the ruling notes. Code and its regression tests go in the first, the per-card notes in the second. August 19 alone has 63 commits.

Card effects were transcribed against a structured card dataset. The data is used only by a read-only audit tool during development and is never read at runtime. The audit matched the name, icons, and faction of all 63 cards, counted 312 effect objects recursively, and found 48 cards whose quantity differed from the local list. The audit only reports that and never imports it; the local list is the authority for physical counts. Where the data was ambiguous or wrong, the card art and the official rules decided. A ruling the official documents leave open is not decided in code; it goes into the open-questions list first.

On August 26 the baseline was 506 pytest tests, action codec v52, and 3,111 rows in the base-ruleset action list, with 47 of the 50 base Imperium cards done. The last three were finished on August 26–27.

## CHOAM contracts and the 20-contract correction

CHOAM contract market, the completion conditions of standard contracts, and the CHOAM-only Imperium cards were implemented on August 27–28. On August 30 the composition of the 20 standard contracts was corrected. The official six-player supplement's CHOAM setup says to set the two Sardaukar contracts aside, so both belong to the standard 20. The by-space composition then sums to exactly 20 only if a third High Council tile is not included, and that tile was a Rise of Ix tile that had been mistaken for a standard one. The same commit wired up Sardaukar II's Agent recall reward. By the glossary the Agent just sent is not a valid target (`[Main p. 20]`), so with no other placed Agent the reward does nothing.

## Changing the structure for Intrigue

Adding 44 Intrigue cards (39 identities) the same way would have doubled the "grows with the card count" problem, so on August 28 the structure was reorganized first (plan document `refactoring-plan.md`).

- **A kind on decision frames:** code that identified frames by string patterns now compares a kind (`FrameKind`), and the legal-action providers and action handlers are driven by tables per kind. The legal-action lists of the old and new engines were compared over 240 random games (about 160,000 decision points) and were identical except in one place, where the old approach had been hiding an implemented card (Smuggler's Haven). The codec stayed the same and the tests went from 573 to 577.
- **A typed effect DSL:** instead of one named effect per card (about 55 of them), a small structure combining conditions, costs, rewards, and choices, with a general interpreter, was built, and Intrigue was moved onto it. Moving the existing Imperium effects to the DSL was not started.
- **Throughput:** an input-immutability check that compared hashes of the whole state (including the growing event log) before and after every transition made random play grow with the square of the game length. Making it opt-in raised random play from about 69 to about 3,200 steps per second (about 45 times). The check stays on only for the kernel test engines.

## Finishing the Intrigue deck, and where the rules are silent

On August 28–29 all 39 Intrigue identities were transcribed. They include Plot, Combat, and Endgame cards, and new needs: cards that stay face up during a game and keep acting, and an end-of-game window. Where the official documents do not decide, a project convention was written down with a number and its reasoning. OQ-001 covers the Endgame Intrigue window (one pass around the table starting from the first player, with wild battle-icon matching chosen inside the window), OQ-003 the participation eligibility when a Combat Intrigue changes troops, and OQ-016 the lifetime of face-up Intrigue. On August 29 a random four-player run of 60 games finished for the first time (240 Endgame windows, 27 wild matches, replay verification passing).

The nine leaders (eight base-game leaders and the CHOAM-only Shaddam) were transcribed on August 29–30, checked against the card art. New rulings OQ-017 to OQ-021 resulted, for instance that Feyd's token on the rightmost space pays nothing (OQ-017) and that Other Memories may be used with zero memories (OQ-018). OQ-021 (Shaddam once the market is empty) was ruled again on September 2: while set-aside Sardaukar contracts remain, he chooses between 2 Solari and such a contract even if the market is empty.

## A review finding applied without checking the rule

On August 28 a code review reported that "Spice gained from Intrigue contaminates the Harvest contract check", and the change that removed that Spice from the total was committed without opening the rules documents. But the documents already said that the contract completes if the shown amount of Spice is gained during the turn from all sources combined (`[Main p. 16]`). It was reverted soon after, and the cause, mistaking the review's wording for a rule source, was recorded in `docs/lessons.md`. As a result the repository instructions now require that any change to rule behavior (a bug fix, a review finding, a test expectation) first check the matching sentence and citation in `docs/rules/` and state that basis in the commit or test. If the documents are silent, nothing is implemented and the question goes to the open-questions list.

## A whole game as a learning environment

On August 30 a runner that plays a whole game was connected to the PettingZoo environment `dune_imperium_uprising_v1`, which changed from one round to one whole game per episode (design document `rl-environment.md`).

- **Observation:** a flat vector of 1,409 integers built only from one player's `PlayerView` (observation v1). Opponent seats are rotated relative to the observer so that all seats can share weights in self-play. The counts of opponents' hand, deck, discard pile, and Intrigue cards are public, as on a physical table (a partial convention under OQ-010); which cards they are stays hidden.
- **Reward:** given only when the game ends. The official tiebreaks always produce one winner, who gets +1 while the others get −1/3 each, so the sum is zero. Intermediate victory-point rewards were removed from the environment; the final information carries each seat's rank and victory points so the training side can shape rewards itself.
- **Chance:** shuffles and similar events are resolved inside the environment from a seed, so the acting side is called only at choice points.

Twelve base games and six CHOAM games were completed through the environment and the zero-sum reward was confirmed (about 4,100 agent steps per second). In the process, an error was found and fixed where a Spy recalled by Espionage was consumed first by another freely ordered effect and the placement failed. There were 755 tests. These 18 games confirm the reward structure; as evidence that the rules are right they are small. A 10,000-game-scale check built later the same day found more hidden errors, and that is the subject of [the next entry]({{< relref path="devlog/2026-08-30-random-sweep.md" lang="en" >}}).

## What remained

Moving the Imperium card effects to the DSL, splitting test files, and similar items were not started. Some of the 22 board spaces (four in the base ruleset and one in CHOAM) were also not yet implemented and were filled in on September 1.
