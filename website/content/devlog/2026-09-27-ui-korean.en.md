## What needed fixing

Games with friends are played on the browser screen (`server/static/`). By mid-September it worked, but the pieces on the board did not match their printed places, the legal actions of an Agent turn came out one per line as the list the AI trains on, and Korean and English were mixed within sentences. This post collects the work on that screen from September 18 to 27. Engine rules, the action codec, and the training code were not changed by it (the server change in the turn-end section is described separately).

## Fitting board pieces to their printed places

The board scan prints circles for the score track and High Council seats, squares on the influence tracks, and a border on every space. Sizes and positions of pieces were taken from values measured on the scan instead of by eye.

- **Discs.** The score marker and the Councilor token are one common disc. Its size is the diameter of the disc position printed on each scan (176/6012 px on the main board, 324/5551 px on the Bene Tleilax board).
- **Influence cubes.** The 16 squares the strips print at Influence 0 all measured 95/6012 px (1.58%), and the cubes were sized to match. In the process, the vertical offsets between strips (24.3, 48.7, 73.2) turned out to be wrong and became 24.56, 49.08, 73.72. The measured deviation of the cubes' four edges is at most 0.054%.
- **Space borders.** The white borders of all 22 spaces measured 457×354 px (standard deviation under 1 px). The hand-measured boxes used before differed in size from space to space, so the highlight borders were uneven. A stylesheet specificity bug that painted a whole box when the mouse merely hovered over it was found at the same time.
- **Pieces.** Agents and Spies are drawn as the silhouettes of the rulebook icons (a hooded figure for the Agent, a cylinder for the Spy) instead of numbered circles, with overlap ratios (IoU) of 0.98 and 0.99. Observation post centres were re-measured by fitting circles to the two printed rings; the earlier hand measurement was off by up to 0.58.
- **Missing pieces.** The Shield Wall token, the Research Station overlay (the catalogue had no Immortality variant, so Immortality games showed the base picture), and the Tuek's Sietch space, which exists only when Esmar Tuek is in the game, were added.

Replacing a picture under the same name kept showing the old one; that was fixed by appending a version value built from the file's modification time and size to the picture's address. These picture files are used locally and are not committed to the repository; without a file the screen falls back to plain shapes.

On the evening of the 27th the Conflict's units also became pieces instead of a number. Each unit gets a piece inside the seat's printed quadrant (troops as cubes in the seat colour), and garrison troops fill the printed circle in their actual number. The board no longer shrinks below 480 px, and narrower windows scroll sideways; an automatic overlap check runs at three window sizes (1600×1000, 1000×900, 800×900).

## Splitting the Agent turn into steps

The legal actions of an Agent turn are a flat list combining card, space, cost choice, and Graft partner. That suits AI training but not people, so only the screen was changed to choose the card first, then the space, then what remains. The engine and codec are untouched: the screen filters the same list and finally sends one action number. Choosing a card lights up the spaces it can reach, and choosing a space first works too. Pressing the same thing again or Esc cancels.

For Graft I first wrote that the server would have to precompute things before two cards could be picked up front, which was wrong. The rule is that an Agent can be sent using either card's Agent icon and both count as having sent an Agent (`[Immortality p. 10]`), and the engine already lists Graft placements per card. The spaces reachable with two cards are the union of the two cards' placements, so the screen can compute them from the list it already has. Picking two cards first was built that way.

Two more things went in that day. One is the troop stepper: actions that differ only in count (`deploy_troops` 1, 2, 3…) fold into one line with a number control, and the server previews the combat strength after the action (`strength_after`). The other is the Reveal purchase panel, which shows the remaining Persuasion and what was bought this Reveal. The next day, games with only AI seats could be watched with automatic playback at an interval of 0.25–4 s. The first run of the new E2E `staged_turn.py` (31 and then 42 checks) caught a real defect: in full-list mode the toggle was hidden behind the log panel and could not be clicked.

## UI improvement plan, stages 0–10

On September 20 the whole screen was audited from several viewpoints and measured, and a plan document was written. Of the 61 audit findings, 29 were rejected on rebuttal; the rest were carried out in stages in an order agreed on beforehand.

- **Stages 0–1: defects and structure.** Creating a game with only the Tech Module on failed with HTTP 500; it now returns 400 with a message. Below 1700 px, the side column jumped to the top whenever another seat moved, because the column was missing from the list of panes whose scroll position is kept. The `app.js` of more than 5,000 lines was cut into nine files, and the same 5,064 non-empty lines were confirmed before and after.
- **Stage 2: a Korean glossary.** Terms from the official Korean rulebook were matched page for page against the English one to build 117 glossary rows. Rules terms use only glossary words, and names of cards and spaces are not invented. The old way of finding icons with 29 English regular expressions made icons vanish the moment text was translated, so the server and the label layer now emit term tokens. Mixed Korean–English rows in the label tables went from 195 to 0, and English event lines in Korean logs went from 1,438 of 3,572 (40%, three full-expansion games) to 0. The bug where a card name turned into an icon, showing `배치 — , Arrakeen`, also disappeared.
- **Stage 3: layout.** The shared card columns fold open only when needed (`c` key). The Bene Tleilax board, shrunk 33 times, opens with "enlarge" at a 5.6-times reduction with 115 px research spaces. Seat panels fold the card rows into "details", cutting the late-game height from 2,189 px to 936 px.
- **Stages 4–6: test net and English.** The E2E scripts, which did not look at expansion games, finished games, or a second resolution, were widened from 12 scripts to 24 by stage 8. The end screen states the VP gap to second place, or the first tiebreak that separated equal VP. The English / 한국어 button in the header must, when switched to English, leave no Hangul anywhere (apart from the button itself) and return to the identical screen when pressed again; `lang.py` checks text, title, alt, and aria-label for all of it. The 273 hard-coded Korean strings in code moved into a string table (323 entries). The 277 places where engine ids leaked into the log and action list were turned into readable names by one value interpreter. On a 1366×768 laptop a seat shrank from 171 px to 111 px, so all four seats fit.
- **Stages 7–9: field audit.** The log now follows the game, runs of Intrigue passes fold into one card, a one-line summary shows the Conflict's standings and your reward, and deleting a save takes two presses. On leader cards, Feyd and Chani track tokens and the four Navigation card slots of Steersman Y'rkoon are drawn; hidden cards appear only as the server already conceals them.
- **Stage 10: effect text.** Korean twins (`*_ko`) were added for the 514 kinds of English effect text generated from engine data, so card, contract, and Conflict effects appear in Korean on the Korean screen.

One bug turned up while playing. With a Hangul input method on, the same physical key arrives as `event.key` "ㅊ" or "ㄴ" rather than Latin `c` or `s`, so the shortcuts `c` and `s` worked only with an English keyboard. In the developer's own browser the keyboard was in English, so it looked fine. The comparison now uses the physical key code (`KeyC`, `KeyS`), and the E2E fires an event the way a Hangul keyboard does (key "ㅊ", code `KeyC`) and was confirmed to fail on the earlier code.

## Ending a turn takes exactly one press

This had been a nuisance before as well. At the last leader pick the game moved on before there was time to press anything, and at other times pressing "end Agent turn" was followed by a second "confirm end of turn". Counting 216 human-seat hand-overs in 6 games showed presses scattered across 0, 1 and 2. The cause was the stop condition set on September 3 (the next decision belongs to another seat and the undo window is open).

The screen rule, a project convention outside the rulebook, was set as follows. Every turn end of a human seat is exactly one press. The engine's explicit end actions (`finish_agent_turn`, `finish_reveal`, the combat and Endgame Intrigue passes) are that one press; when a seat's share ends any other way, the server stops and waits for confirmation. One "End turn ▶" button always stands in the same place. The decision uses `open_units`, which records per seat the first step taken inside its own share after the last press, and the saved document keeps the waiting-for-confirmation state so that it returns identically after loading.

The first implementation was replaced by the `open_units` design after an independent review reproduced three defects. Verification: 1,935 pytest tests (26 added) and 16 E2E scripts (the new `turn_end.py` has 67 checks); the new tests were confirmed to fail on each of the six code mutations the review proposed; and an adversarial run of 270 games mixing 1–4 human seats, all expansions, undo, and save/restore found 0 violations. An older defect was fixed the same day: two single-step undos in a row left that game's save impossible to restore. The format was right, but two readers assumed "N entries directly before the marker".

At this stage the server only held the press while the engine still closed turns by itself. That limit led to a third report on September 30 and to engine-side changes in October (see [the October post]({{< relref path="devlog/2026-10-09-turn-end.md" lang="en" >}})).

## Matching verification to risk

Every item in stages 7–10 went through implementation, independent review, fixes, and re-review, and the verification steps took 47% of the working time (running the tests themselves took 21%). Even a small item such as reordering the help text took 40–90 minutes. On September 27 verification was tied to risk. Low-risk changes such as wording, labels, CSS and layout run only the related tests and E2E scripts, and the diff is read directly. Remote synchronisation, hidden information, engine events, undo and save, and the list of offered actions keep an independent review. The full test suite and all E2E scripts run once at the end of a batch.

## What remains open

- On the Korean screen, space names and some card names stay in printed English. Words with no glossary basis (Wild card, Immediate, Usurp, and others) also stay in English.
- At 1366×768 the seat area is 519–652 px, so four folded seats do not always fit.
- The engine-side turn-end problem and the screen changes after it are in the October post.
